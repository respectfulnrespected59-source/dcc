— 03 / The rules that keep everyone's account alive

Read from the platforms' own terms, not from vibes.

All three platforms enforce these, and a pod that breaks one gets every member suspended at once. So they are the sprint's rules too.

Never
One account per person. No shared logins. Ever.Upwork, Fiverr and Freelancer.com each allow exactly one account per human and ban letting anyone else work inside it. The pod shares tools and leads — never a login.
Never
Nothing off-platform with a client you met on-platform.Upwork and Freelancer.com bind you for 24 months after the introduction and charge a conversion fee in the thousands; Fiverr bans off-platform payment outright. Upwork's rule extends to your teammates.
Always
Shared tools are bought as team seats, one per member.Higgsfield's terms forbid sharing a login, so the pool buys a Team or Scale workspace and gives each member a seat. Atlas Cloud gives each pod its own team key so spend is visible per person.
Always
Log every night, zeros included.The pod only reads if everyone logs. Three silent days shows on the pod page — not as shame, as a hazard the coach can act on.
Always
Answer screener questions honestly.Work Scout refuses a posting when its own words disqualify you and tells you why. Nobody in a pod inflates years, certs or clearances to get through a gate. That is the brand.
— 04 / The fee, honestly
DCC's rule is never pay to work.
The sprint fee is not that.

It is a tool co-op. The pooled membership buys the seats on AI tools you would otherwise buy alone, at team rates. Nobody pays DCC to bid, to apply, or to be seen. The amount is set by the cohort before day one, published up front with the refund terms, and held by the organiser's entity — not in anyone's personal account.

What it buys
  • A seat on the pod's shared AI workspaces (video, image, models) for the 30 days
  • A pod with a coach, a lane board and a nightly instrument
  • A founding DCC contractor badge when the platform opens
  • Not a gig, a client, or a guaranteed dollar — the platforms' own fees (0–20%) still apply to what you win
  • Not anyone else's login, on any platform, for any reason

— 06 / Questions

The fine print, in plain words.

Who can see my numbers?

Your pod's coach can read your nightly log while you are in the pod — that is what joining grants, and leaving revokes it instantly. Other members see who is working (touches, replies, leads, nights logged), never what anyone banked. Only the pooled total is shared.

I do not have a Fiverr or Upwork account yet.

Make your own before day one. Each platform allows exactly one account per person, in your own name, and the sprint never creates or shares one for you. Freelancer.com's Basic plan is about $5/month; Upwork and Fiverr are free to open.

Can a pod bid as one agency?

On Upwork, yes — as a real Agency account ($19.99/month), where every member still holds their own linked account and the agency owner becomes the payer of record for the team. Fiverr agencies are invitation-only and need three in-house professionals, so Fiverr runs on individual seller accounts. Neither is required for the sprint; it is a decision each pod makes with its coach.

What does the 30-day target mean?

Each pod sets a pooled dollar target for day 30. The pod page refuses to project a pace for the first five days — one good night times thirty is not a forecast — then shows whether the pod is on pace, behind, and what per-day number closes the gap. It is a direction, never a promise.
